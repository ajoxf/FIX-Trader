The loop
—
Master switches
An unanswered shutdown prompt means no. Our own working orders are cancelled at startup and at shutdown either way.
Guards a guard withholds an order, never a close
Staleness is measured on the quote changing, not on messages arriving: a venue republishing an unchanged book is not a live market. 0 turns the guard off, which also stops it catching a feed that has genuinely stopped.
Desk limits
A desk limit turns every algo off and names the limit. A contract's own limit turns off only that contract.
Defaults for a new contract a blank box on a contract falls back here
Signal when it enters, when it leaves, and when it gives up
Leave Exit mode on Profit target unless you have a reason: it is the one exit that knows what the trade cost. The stop-loss is a z in every mode and is not optional — it is the only exit that fires when the spread keeps going. Lower the entry threshold only against the replay in Analysis: an inner band reverts more often and its move often cannot cover the round trip.
Filters these WITHHOLD entries — none of them ever stops a close
Leave the edge filter ON. It is the one that refuses a trade whose expected move cannot pay for itself. Hurst ships OFF and should stay off until you have watched it — it is an estimate, and on a coarsely quantised spread it reads high, so arming it can withhold every entry on a contract for ever. The half-life filter is off for the same reason. Both are visible on each window whether they are armed or not: read them first, arm them later. A filter that withholds everything says so on the window, in the words of the number that failed.
Size & risk the ceilings — the only settings here that can stop a bad day getting worse
0 means no limit on every one of these, which is why they are worth setting even when they feel generous. A daily loss limit turns that contract's algo off and says so on the window — it does not close what is open. The cooldown is what stops a contract sitting on its threshold and re-entering on every pass.
Execution how an order is sent, and what happens when it is not filled
Entry limit, exit market is the pairing to keep: patience going in costs you a trade you did not take, impatience coming out costs you a position you still hold. Leave on an entry at Cancel and on an exit at Cross at market for the same reason. The close flag is the one setting here that can put a position ON: on a venue that keeps long and short apart, a closing order sent with the wrong flag opens the other side. Change it only when the broker has told you which one that contract wants.
Costs every one of these feeds the edge filter AND the profit target
Get these right before you arm anything. They are not reporting — the edge filter refuses trades against them and the profit target is measured from them, so a commission left at 0 makes every spread look worth trading and sets a target that does not cover the round trip. The slippage budget is a guess to start with: Analysis shows what was actually measured beside it, and proposes a corrected number. Initial margin is the target basis to use; where the broker does not report margin the target renders as — rather than a figure nothing stands behind.
